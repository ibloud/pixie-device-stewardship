# Field Investigation: Power, Platforms, and the Price of Success

**Status:** Research framing / unpublished draft.
**Related investigation:** Lucian Grainge strategic history simulation (Holdings); fame, identity, and ambition inquiry (Mayhem).

## Question

When a person or organization rises through an industry, does it become the villain—or does the surrounding system make concentration, gatekeeping, and dependency appear to be the natural price of success?

We will use the music industry and Lucian Grainge's documented career as a case study, without presuming guilt or innocence. The aim is to understand how power is accumulated, exercised, legitimized, and distributed.

## Atmosphere lens

Our concern is structural: who owns the infrastructure through which creative work is discovered, distributed, monetized, and remembered?

Investigate:
- ownership and control of catalogs, metadata, identity, distribution, audience relationships, and access
- platform and infrastructure dependencies
- bargaining asymmetry between creators and institutions
- interoperability, portability, and the practical ability to exit
- consolidation: efficiencies, new capabilities, concentration, and potential harms
- whether alternatives remain viable after a dominant organization succeeds

## Avoid the easy story

Do not reduce the investigation to an executive as a cartoon antagonist. Separate:
- documented action from inferred motive
- the individual's choices from the incentives and governance structures around them
- commercial outcome from cultural outcome
- market power from demonstrated abuse
- creator agency from institutional constraints

Include evidence that complicates the thesis. Ask what artists, employees, partners, audiences, competitors, and independent operators experienced.

## Research protocol

For each event, preserve a timeline, source links, source date, claim, confidence, counterevidence, affected stakeholders, and unanswered questions. Use primary material wherever practical. Do not publish a conclusion until the evidence and competing explanations are visible.

## Loptr Lab connection

The investigation should inform—but not dictate—our choices around Atmosphere, AT Protocol, PDS, hosting, distribution, and creator-owned systems. Our design test is whether we increase users' agency and portability, or reproduce the dependency we criticize.

The guiding question: **Can infrastructure make success less dependent on surrendering control?**
