Beyond the Personal Data Server

A growth path for independent infrastructure without recreating centralised dependency.

Sovereignty is not measured by how many servers we own. It is measured by the choices we retain when a service changes, becomes unaffordable or disappears.

This continues Your PDS, Your Terms and our Artist Node research. The existing infrastructure working plan separates identity, public delivery, private creative work and game services. We should grow each according to actual demand rather than put every workload behind one server.

Grow through evidence

  1. Establish: rehearse deployment and recovery with a disposable identity, then use Daddy's Little Mortis as the reference artist identity. Record costs and tested recovery.
  2. Validate: measure interoperability across PIXIE, Made-Sick, Play It Forward and Atmosphere without requiring participants to move their accounts.
  3. Collaborate: invite a bounded voluntary pilot only when maintenance, support, safety and exit procedures are funded and tested.
  4. Federate: cooperate with independent operators; use interoperable services and avoid a single operator or provider becoming indispensable.
  5. Sustain: establish transparent governance, reserve funding, compensated maintenance and succession before taking responsibility for more identities.

Capacity before commitments

Record storage and blob growth, traffic, operator hours, incidents, backup restoration times and accessible support needs. Compare 12-, 24- and 36-month costs across managed, owned and hybrid options using actual measurements. Expansion stops if recovery, moderation, staffing or budget cannot support it. Do not buy equipment or move primary identities merely to meet a timetable.

Partnerships as shared capability

We invite infrastructure operators including Blacksky, Eurosky, Northsky and Greenhost to critique these requirements, not to endorse our project. Sliplane is another candidate for a deployment comparison. No provider has been selected. Discussion should progress from public review to a shared problem, a reversible pilot and only then documented responsibilities and exit terms.

Questions for review

What minimum recovery evidence would you require before admitting another account? Which operating costs are easily underestimated? How can independent nodes cooperate without recreating a central gatekeeper?
