Protecting Creative Work on an Independent Budget

Dominique Devereaux · Loptr Lab

A musician can develop a world through songs, clothes, recurring characters and art. A name or symbol that begins in a story may later appear on a product label. When that happens, the creator needs to know who made the artwork, who controls its use, and which questions warrant a lawyer’s review before money goes into a release.

Pay people for the work they do

Mike Monteiro’s CreativeMornings talk Fuck You, Pay Me grounds this discussion in getting paid for creative work and seeking counsel when the stakes merit it. For an independent studio, a lawyer who understands the work can help define the actual engagement, identify what needs judgment, and explain which deliverables the fee covers. A creator can prepare the records in advance and ask for a quote tied to a specific question.

A production template can help capture an agreed deal. Thoolie offers lawyer-drafted film templates and describes limits on individualized advice. Preserve signed and dated agreements and verify that the contract covers the intended medium and use. Thanks Mom describes a separate service focused on U.S. trademark clearance. A search, a filing, an ownership agreement and defense of a dispute each have their own scope and cost.

What the courts decided

  • Jack Daniel’s Properties v. VIP Products (U.S. Supreme Court, 2023). When the challenged use functions as branding for the seller’s own goods, the ordinary trademark confusion inquiry applies. Parody can still matter in that inquiry. Review how a symbol appears on packaging, merchandise and store pages. Supreme Court opinion.
  • DC Comics v. Towle (Ninth Circuit, 2015). The court recognized copyright in the Batmobile as a sufficiently distinctive character on the record before it. Compare specific design features and document authorship and permissions before drawing conclusions from a visual resemblance. Appellate opinion.

For commissioned artwork, record the exact asset and use, then verify the signed license or assignment. U.S. copyright transfers generally require a signed writing. Commissioned work qualifies as work made for hire only in limited circumstances. 17 U.S.C. §§ 201 and 204 · Copyright Office work-made-for-hire guidance.

Where public policy could help

The Copyright Claims Board handles eligible copyright disputes up to $30,000 and respondents can opt out. Trademark clearance and trademark disputes fall outside that route. A practical policy question is whether early, affordable advice and clearer access to dispute information help creators preserve their rights. Copyright Claims Board · Loptr Lab’s policy research and public review.

What would count as evidence of savings?

Compare equivalent project briefs prepared with and without a structured creator-side record. Track preparation time, legal review cost when it occurs, omissions spotted in review, and questions that still required professional judgment. Include rework and accessibility barriers in the total. A shorter intake that increases missed ownership or permission questions fails the test. Any savings remain a hypothesis.

For a budget reference, the USPTO base application fee is $350 per class, with additional fees possible. Its Minnesota resource page lists trademark clinics, subject to intake and eligibility. The USPTO likelihood-of-confusion guidance explains why the mark and the related goods and services both matter.
